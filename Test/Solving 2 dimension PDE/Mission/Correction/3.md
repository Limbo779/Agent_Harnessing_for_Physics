### Step 1: Decoupled Vertical ODE

The 1D time-independent Schrödinger equation governing vertical motion under a uniform linear potential $V(y) = mgy$ for $y \ge 0$ is:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2 Y(y)}{dy^2} + mgy Y(y) = E_y Y(y) \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

Factoring out $mg$ from the spatial and energy terms yields:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2 Y(y)}{dy^2} + mg \left(y - \frac{E_y}{mg}\right) Y(y) = 0 \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

---

### Step 2: Dimensionless Variable Transformation

To convert the ODE into canonical dimensionless form, define the scaling constant $\alpha$ and dimensionless coordinate $z(y)$:

$$\alpha = \left(\frac{2m^2 g}{\hbar^2}\right)^{1/3} \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

$$z(y) = \alpha \left(y - \frac{E_y}{mg}\right) = \left(\frac{2m^2 g}{\hbar^2}\right)^{1/3} \left(y - \frac{E_y}{mg}\right) \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

#### Dimensional Analysis Verification

$$\text{Dim}[\alpha] = \left(\frac{\text{M}^2 \cdot \text{L T}^{-2}}{\text{M}^2 \text{L}^4 \text{T}^{-2}}\right)^{1/3} = (\text{L}^{-3})^{1/3} = \text{L}^{-1} \quad \text{[cite: 17, 18, 19]}$$

$$\text{Dim}\left[y - \frac{E_y}{mg}\right] = \text{L} \implies \text{Dim}[z(y)] = \text{L}^{-1} \cdot \text{L} = 1 \quad \text{(Strictly unitless)} \quad \text{[cite: 17, 18, 19]}$$

Applying the chain rule to transform spatial derivatives with respect to $y$ into derivatives with respect to $z$:

$$\frac{dY}{dy} = \frac{dY}{dz} \frac{dz}{dy} = \alpha \frac{dY}{dz} \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

$$\frac{d^2 Y}{dy^2} = \alpha^2 \frac{d^2 Y}{dz^2} = \left(\frac{2m^2 g}{\hbar^2}\right)^{2/3} \frac{d^2 Y}{dz^2} \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

### Step 3: Conversion to Airy's Canonical Equation

Substituting $\frac{d^2 Y}{dy^2}$ and $\left(y - \frac{E_y}{mg}\right) = \frac{z}{\alpha}$ into the rearranged Schrödinger equation:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \left(\frac{2m^2 g}{\hbar^2}\right)^{2/3} \frac{d^2 Y(z)}{dz^2} + \frac{mg}{\left(\frac{2m^2 g}{\hbar^2}\right)^{1/3}} z Y(z) = 0 \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

Simplifying the coefficients:

1. **First term coefficient:**

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \left(\frac{2m^2 g}{\hbar^2}\right)^{2/3} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \cdot \frac{2^{2/3} m^{4/3} g^{2/3}}{\hbar^{4/3}} = -\left(\frac{m g^2 \hbar^2}{2}\right)^{1/3} \quad \text{[cite: 15, 17, 18]}$$


2. **Second term coefficient:**

$$\frac{mg}{\left(\frac{2m^2 g}{\hbar^2}\right)^{1/3}} = \frac{mg}{\frac{2^{1/3} m^{2/3} g^{1/3}}{\hbar^{2/3}}} = \left(\frac{m g^2 \hbar^2}{2}\right)^{1/3} \quad \text{[cite: 15, 17, 18]}$$



Substituting these back into the differential equation yields:

$$-\left(\frac{m g^2 \hbar^2}{2}\right)^{1/3} \frac{d^2 Y(z)}{dz^2} + \left(\frac{m g^2 \hbar^2}{2}\right)^{1/3} z Y(z) = 0 \quad \text{[cite: 15]}$$

Dividing by $-\left(\frac{m g^2 \hbar^2}{2}\right)^{1/3}$ produces Airy's canonical equation:

$$\frac{d^2 Y(z)}{dz^2} - z Y(z) = 0 \quad \text{[cite: 15]}$$

### Step 4: Asymptotic Boundary Conditions & Selection of Physical Branch

The general solution to Airy's canonical equation is a linear combination of the Airy functions $\text{Ai}(z)$ and $\text{Bi}(z)$:

$$Y(z) = C_1 \text{Ai}(z) + C_2 \text{Bi}(z) \quad \text{[cite: 15]}$$

As $y \to \infty$, $z(y) \to +\infty$. The asymptotic forms for large positive real arguments ($z \gg 0$) derived via steepest descent are:

$$\text{Ai}(z) \sim \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi} z^{1/4}} \exp\left(-\frac{2}{3} z^{3/2}\right) \xrightarrow{z \to +\infty} 0 \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17, 18, 19]}$$

$$\text{Bi}(z) \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi} z^{1/4}} \exp\left(\frac{2}{3} z^{3/2}\right) \xrightarrow{z \to +\infty} \infty \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17, 18, 19]}$$

To ensure that the wave function remains square-integrable and physical as $y \to \infty$:

$$\lim_{y \to \infty} Y(y) = 0 \implies C_2 = 0 \quad \text{[cite: 15]}$$

Thus, the vertical solution branch is:

$$Y(y) = C_y \text{Ai}(z(y)) \quad \text{[cite: 15]}$$

---

### Step 5: Ground Dirichlet Boundary Condition & Energy Quantization

The vertical potential contains an infinite hard wall at ground level ($V(y) = \infty$ for $y < 0$). Wave function continuity requires:

$$Y(0) = 0 \implies C_y \text{Ai}(z(0)) = 0 \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17, 19]}$$

For non-trivial solutions ($C_y \neq 0$), $z(0)$ must coincide with one of the discrete negative zeros $a_n$ of the Airy function $\text{Ai}$ ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$, where $a_n < 0$ and $a_1 \approx -2.3381, a_2 \approx -4.0879$):

$$z(0) = -\left(\frac{2}{m g^2 \hbar^2}\right)^{1/3} E_y = a_n \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17, 19]}$$

Solving for the quantized vertical energy eigenvalues $E_{y,n}$:

$$E_{y,n} = \left(\frac{m g^2 \hbar^2}{2}\right)^{1/3} (-a_n) = \left(\frac{m g^2 \hbar^2}{2}\right)^{1/3} \vert{}a_n\vert{} \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17]}$$

Substituting $z(y)$ in terms of $a_n$ gives the exact, boundary-enforced vertical spatial solution:

$$Y_n(y) = C_y \text{Ai}\left( \left(\frac{2m^2 g}{\hbar^2}\right)^{1/3} y + a_n \right) \quad \text{for } y \ge 0 \quad \text{[cite: 16]}$$

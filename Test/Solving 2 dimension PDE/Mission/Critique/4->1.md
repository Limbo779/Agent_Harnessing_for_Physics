### 2. Flaws & Proven Counter-Derivations

* **Flagged Step 1:** Definition of horizontal wavevector $k_x$ in Section 1 ($k_x = \frac{2mE_x}{\hbar} = \frac{mu \cos\theta}{\hbar}$).


* **Nature of Error:** Algebraic and Dimensional Error.


* **Rigorous Mathematical Proof of Error:**
The horizontal 1D Schrödinger equation derived by Agent 1 is:



$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{d^2 X(x)}{dx^2} = E_x X(x) \implies \frac{d^2 X(x)}{dx^2} + \frac{2m E_x}{\hbar^2} X(x) = 0$$



Comparing this to the Helmholtz equation $\frac{d^2 X(x)}{dx^2} + k_x^2 X(x) = 0$ requires $k_x^2 = \frac{2m E_x}{\hbar^2}$, which gives:



$$k_x = \frac{\sqrt{2m E_x}}{\hbar}$$



Agent 1 omitted the square root over $2m E_x$ and omitted the exponent on $\hbar$ in the explicit formula $k_x = \frac{2m E_x}{\hbar}$. Dimensional analysis confirms this error:



$$\text{Dim}\left[ k_x \right] = \text{L}^{-1}$$


$$\text{Dim}\left[ \frac{2m E_x}{\hbar} \right] = \frac{\text{M} \cdot (\text{M L}^2 \text{T}^{-2})}{\text{M L}^2 \text{T}^{-1}} = \text{M T}^{-1} \neq \text{L}^{-1}$$


$$\text{Dim}\left[ \frac{\sqrt{2m E_x}}{\hbar} \right] = \frac{\sqrt{\text{M}^2 \text{L}^2 \text{T}^{-2}}}{\text{M L}^2 \text{T}^{-1}} = \frac{\text{M L T}^{-1}}{\text{M L}^2 \text{T}^{-1}} = \text{L}^{-1}$$



Although substituting $E_x = \frac{1}{2}m u^2 \cos^2\theta$ into the correct expression yields $\frac{m u \cos\theta}{\hbar}$, the intermediate definition formula $k_x = \frac{2m E_x}{\hbar}$ stated by Agent 1 is mathematically incorrect.


* **Corrected Formulation:**

$$k_x = \frac{\sqrt{2m E_x}}{\hbar} = \frac{\sqrt{2m \left( \frac{1}{2} m u^2 \cos^2\theta \right)}}{\hbar} = \frac{m u \cos\theta}{\hbar}$$

* **Flagged Step 2:** Rigid continuous classical specification of $E_y = \frac{1}{2}m(u \sin\theta)^2$ in the 1D vertical quantum ODE.


* **Nature of Error:** Physical and Boundary Condition Error.


* **Rigorous Mathematical Proof of Error:**
The vertical 1D ODE $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{d^2 Y(y)}{dy^2} + mgy Y(y) = E_y Y(y)$ subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions $Y(0) = 0$ and $\lim_{y \to \infty} Y(y) = 0$ forms a quantum bound-state eigenvalue problem. Its solution requires:



$$\text{Ai}\left( -\left(\frac{2 m E_y^3}{\hbar^2 g^2}\right)^{1/3} \right) = 0 \implies E_{y, n} = \left( \frac{\hbar^2 g^2}{2m} \right)^{1/3} \vert{}a_n\vert{}, \quad n \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$$



Fixing $E_y$ to an arbitrary continuous classical kinetic energy $\frac{1}{2}m u^2 \sin^2\theta$ over-constrains the differential equation. A continuous classical $E_y$ does not generally satisfy $\text{Ai}(z(0)) = 0$, violating the ground boundary condition $Y(0) = 0$. Classical energy $E_{y, \text{class}} = \frac{1}{2} m u^2 \sin^2\theta$ can only be identified with $E_{y, n}$ in the semi-classical (high quantum number $n \gg 1$) limit.


* **Corrected Formulation:**

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2 Y(y)}{dy^2} + mgy Y(y) = E_{y, n} Y(y)$$



where $E_{y, n} = \left(\frac{\hbar^2 g^2}{2m}\right)^{1/3} \vert{}a_n\vert{}$, and $E_{y, \text{class}} = \frac{1}{2} m u^2 \sin^2\theta$ is applied strictly as an asymptotic correspondence constraint for $n \gg 1$.

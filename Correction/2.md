### 1. Decoupled Horizontal ODE

The horizontal motion of the particle is governed by the 1D time-independent Schrödinger equation for a free particle along the $x$-axis:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2 X(x)}{dx^2} = E_x X(x) \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

Rearranging into standard second-order Helmholtz ODE form:

$$\frac{d^2 X(x)}{dx^2} + k_x^2 X(x) = 0$$

where the horizontal wavenumber $k_x$ is rigorously defined as:

$$k_x \equiv \frac{\sqrt{2mE_x}}{\hbar} \quad \left(\text{or equivalently } k_x^2 = \frac{2mE_x}{\hbar^2}\right) \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17, 18, 19]}$$

### 2. Spatial Wave Solution $X(x)$

The general solution to this differential equation in complex exponential form is:

$$X(x) = A e^{i k_x x} + B e^{-i k_x x} \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

For a projectile launched forward in the positive $x$-direction with well-defined positive horizontal momentum $p_x > 0$, set $B = 0$:

$$X(x) = A e^{i k_x x} \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

### 3. Derivation of the Horizontal Wavevector $k_x$

Classically, a particle launched with initial speed $u$ at angle $\theta$ relative to the horizontal ground has a horizontal velocity component:

$$v_x = u \cos\theta \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

The kinetic energy associated with horizontal motion is:

$$E_x = \frac{1}{2} m v_x^2 = \frac{1}{2} m u^2 \cos^2\theta \quad \text{[cite: 14]}$$

Substituting $E_x$ into the corrected expression $k_x = \frac{\sqrt{2mE_x}}{\hbar}$:

$$k_x = \frac{\sqrt{2m \left(\frac{1}{2} m u^2 \cos^2\theta\right)}}{\hbar} = \frac{\sqrt{m^2 u^2 \cos^2\theta}}{\hbar} = \frac{m u \cos\theta}{\hbar} \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17, 18, 19]}$$

#### Dimensional Check

$$\text{Dim}[k_x] = \frac{\text{M} \cdot (\text{L} \cdot \text{T}^{-1})}{\text{M} \cdot \text{L}^2 \cdot \text{T}^{-1}} = \text{L}^{-1} \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17, 18, 19]}$$

This confirms exact consistency with the de Broglie relation $p_x = \hbar k_x = m u \cos\theta$.

### 4. 1D Spatial Probability Density Normalization Integrals

#### Dirac Delta Normalization (Continuous Spectrum)

The inner product normalization condition over the spatial domain $(-\infty, \infty)$ is set up as:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} X_{k_x'}^*(x) X_{k_x}(x) \, dx = \vert{}A\vert{}^2 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{i(k_x - k_x')x} \, dx = \vert{}A\vert{}^2 \cdot 2\pi \delta(k_x - k_x') = \delta(k_x - k_x') \quad \text{[cite: 15]}$$

Matching coefficients gives:

$$2\pi \vert{}A\vert{}^2 = 1 \implies \vert{}A\vert{}^2 = \frac{1}{2\pi} \implies \vert{}A\vert{} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17, 18, 19]}$$

$$X(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{i k_x x} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{i \left(\frac{m u \cos\theta}{\hbar}\right) x} \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17, 18, 19]}$$

#### Box Normalization (Finite Length $L$)

Restricting the horizontal domain to a normalization box of length $L$:

$$\int_{0}^{L} \vert{}X(x)\vert{}^2 \, dx = \vert{}A\vert{}^2 \int_{0}^{L} dx = \vert{}A\vert{}^2 L = 1 \quad \text{[cite: 15]}$$

Solving for $A$:

$$\vert{}A\vert{}^2 = \frac{1}{L} \implies A = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17, 18, 19]}$$

$$X(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} e^{i k_x x} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}} e^{i \left(\frac{m u \cos\theta}{\hbar}\right) x} \quad \text{[cite: 16, 17, 18, 19]}$$

#### Dimensional Consistency

For a 1D spatial wave function, $\int \vert{}X(x)\vert{}^2 dx = 1 \implies [X(x)] = \text{L}^{-1/2}$. When combined with the vertical wave function $Y(y)$ ($[Y(y)] = \text{L}^{-1/2}$), the overall 2D spatial wave function $\psi(x,y) = X(x)Y(y)$ satisfies the required spatial dimension $[\psi(x,y)] = \text{L}^{-1}$.
